# Canvas Architecture

**Audience:** developers changing the space page in Collaborator: the canvas, and the modules on it (the conference, the chat and installed modules).

How a space's canvas is built and the rules that keep it predictable. The conference and the chat are built-in modules, handled the same way as an installed one. Wider than a phone they are not columns of the canvas grid but live in the always-on column beside it ("The work area and the always-on column" below); the grid rules in this document apply to them on a phone, floating and in a window of their own. What a player sees is in [userguide-call](../userguides/userguide-call.md); colors are in [design-theme](../designsystem/design-theme.md).

## The grid

The canvas (`#canvas` in `public/space.html`) is a CSS grid. A docked **installed module** owns a column and its content fills the whole of it: a module has no bar in the grid's bottom row, since its add actions are the titlebar's **+** and its counts the status line at the foot of its own window ([architecture-module-window](architecture-module-window.md)). Only the conference and the chat have a bottom bar (`.mod-bar`: the call's toolbar and the chat's message box), and they use the grid only where the layout is a phone's, or they are floating or in a window of their own; wider than a phone they live in the always-on column beside the canvas (below). Where the bars exist they share the bottom row, so they line up, and that row is as tall as the tallest bar. Nothing is measured.

```
+-------------------------------+--------------------+
| video content                 | chat content       |
+-------------------------------+--------------------+
| video bar (toolbar)           | chat bar (format,  |
|                               | message box)       |
+-------------------------------+--------------------+
```

- **Columns.** One column per docked module, in order: the conference, the chat, then installed modules. `syncDock()` in `public/canvas.js` sets the whole template as `--canvas-cols`: one column is flexible (`minmax(0, 1fr)`) and the others are fixed widths. The flexible column is the conference when it is docked, else the first docked module, so the canvas never has an empty column. `.is-flex` marks it and hides its resize handle.
- **Rows.** `minmax(0, 1fr)` for content, then the bottom row for the conference's and the chat's bars (`--bar-h` and the bottom safe-area inset). An installed module's content spans both rows (`grid-row: 1 / -1`).
- **Modules.** A `.module` element has `display: contents`, so its content and bar become items of the canvas grid, placed by `grid-column` and `grid-row`. Video is `.module-video`, chat is `.module-chat` (the `#chat` element).
- **Closing a module** hides it and removes its column, so the others take the width. With nothing open the canvas shows `#canvas-empty`.

**When the columns do not fit.** If the fixed columns together are wider than the canvas leaves them (the canvas minus the conference's minimum), `syncDock()` shows them squeezed in step and the conference stays at its minimum. A drag would then change a stored width that is not what is on screen, so `settleDock()` (called as a drag starts) gives each fixed module the width it is showing, and `takeWidthFromOthers()` takes the width for a widening column from the other fixed columns, not from the conference or from the column being dragged. Narrowing a column then gives the width to the conference.

**How many columns fit** ([plan-docked-limit](../plans/plan-docked-limit.md), GitHub #156). Every column keeps a least width: the flexible column 280px (`FLEX_MIN`), every other 240px (`DOCK_MIN`, the old floor was 160). `dockLimit(width)` in `public/snap-grid.js` gives how many columns a canvas that wide holds: 2 from 641 to 759px, 3 from 760, 4 from 1000, 5 from 1280, 6 from 1600 and 7 from 1920; at 640px and below (`NARROW`, the phone layout of both the space bar and the canvas) there is no limit, since one view shows at a time. `fitDock(width, columns)` decides who gets a column: the conference and the chat always keep theirs, and installed modules give way from the right. A module that wants a column and has none **waits** (`mod.waiting`): it is shown floating, its remembered mode stays `dock`, and as soon as the canvas is wide enough it docks again in its old place in the column order, at its own width. Dragging or resizing a waiting module makes it floating for good. On a waiting module the titlebar's Dock is dimmed, titled "Docks when the window is wide enough"; on another floating module while the columns are full it reads "The window is too narrow for another column". The conference's and the chat's Dock always works: when the columns are full, the rightmost installed module waits instead. `dockAll()` goes through the same fit and returns `{ docked, waiting }`. `createCanvas({ onNote })` takes a function for a short line in the Layout menu (see [architecture-navigation](architecture-navigation.md), "The module chooser (Layout)"): it is told when a module the person docked or opened waits, and after Panel Mode docks, never on entering, a resize or a pop-out. Floating, a module in its own window and phones are unchanged. `tools/check-canvas.mjs` holds `dockLimit` and `fitDock`.

**Reordering.** A docked module is dragged by its titlebar (`wireReorder()` in `public/canvas.js`; buttons in the bar still click, and a press that does not move is a click). While it moves, `.canvas.reordering` is set and the module's parts wear `.module-lifted`; the pointer's place among the other columns (their widths read from the canvas's computed `grid-template-columns`) gives the new index, the docked modules' `order` values are reassigned in that sequence, and `syncDock()` moves the columns live. The order is remembered with the layout (`__open`), and `restore()` applies it to every module, the conference and the chat included. It does nothing on a narrow canvas, where the modules are tabs.

The chat's resize handle (`#chat-resize`) is its own grid item spanning both rows, on the chat column's left edge, so dragging it moves the content and the bar together.

## Docked and floating

The conference's and the chat's bars are cells in the bottom row of their column. In the popped-out window (`body.popout`) the conference keeps its titlebar and its toolbar, and both slide away when the canvas goes `.idle` (nothing has moved for a moment): the titlebar up, the toolbar down. Idle takes them out of the flow (`position: absolute` plus a transform), so the tiles take the whole window, and the next movement slides them back. The chat bar stays docked, because the message box needs a fixed place.

A module that is *floating* (over the page, in the floating layer) or in a window of its own has its content stacked in that floating frame or window, with the toolbar and status line the module asked for and no bar of its own.

**Free or snapped.** Floating is free by default: anywhere over the page, any size, the box (`{ x, y, w, h }`) remembered per module. A floating module's titlebar has a toggle (`[data-snap]`, `aria-pressed`) that makes it *snap* instead: it sits in the cells of a grid laid over the canvas (as many cells as the canvas fits at the grid's pitch, never fewer than one, with a 4px gutter (`SNAP_GAP`, which the grid keeps half of at the Workspace's edges, so a window at an edge sits a gutter from it); the whole window when the canvas is not shown; a module never takes fewer cells than its smallest size needs), dragging moves it a cell at a time, resizing grows it a cell at a time, and what is remembered is its cells (`cell: { col, row, cols, rows }`, beside the box), so a window resize re-tiles it into the grid the new size makes rather than just keeping it on screen. Turning snap on settles the module into the cells nearest its box at once; turning it off leaves it where it is, free again. The grid itself (`.snap-grid` in the floating layer, sized to the canvas, the cell pitch in `--snap-cw`/`--snap-ch`) is drawn only while a snapped module is being dragged or resized (`.module-layer.snapping`). The grid's arithmetic has no DOM and lives in `public/snap-grid.js`, so `tools/check-canvas.mjs` can run it: `gridFor` (the grid a canvas and a pitch make), `cellBox` and `snapCell` (a run of cells to a box and back), `leastSpan` and `clampCell` (never fewer cells than the smallest size, 240 by 160px, needs), `cellsOverlap`, `nearestFree` and `nextFree`, `tileFresh` (sharing the grid among modules nobody has placed) and `resettle` (below), with `MIN_W`, `MIN_H` and `SNAP_GAP`. `public/canvas.js` measures the canvas (`snapGrid`) and places the modules (`settleSnap`, `applySnapLayout`, `setSnapPitch`). **Panel Mode and Window Mode** (Thomas, 2026-10-13) are how the Workspace holds its modules, chosen in the Layout menu's Arrange section and remembered per person per space (`__mode`; `arrangeMode()`, `setArrangeMode()` in `public/canvas.js`). In Panel Mode, modules are docked, columns beside the call, and a module opened later docks (`preferredMode`); choosing it docks every floating module that can dock (`dockAll()`). In Window Mode they float as windows over the Workspace and a module opened later floats; choosing it floats every docked module that can float, each at its last floating place where it has one and the rest tidied around them (`tidyBoxes`), and with the grid on they snap. A saved layout keeps each module's own mode and loading one sets the mode to what it holds (all floating: Window Mode; else Panel Mode); a phone has one view at a time and no mode. Only in Window Mode does the menu offer two canvas-level controls (the menu's **Snap to grid** switch and **Grid size** slider, wired to `canvas.snapAll`/`setSnapPitch`; see [architecture-navigation](architecture-navigation.md), "The module chooser (Layout)"): a switch that puts every module that can float onto the grid: the docked ones are floated first (their docked mode remembered in `__snap.before`) and every floating module snaps, each one's own switch following; any module opened later opens floating and snapped (`preferredMode`, `api_openBuiltin`); turned off, the modules that were docked when it went on dock again and the rest stay floating, free. A module on a narrow canvas (a phone) is left docked either way. And, while it is on, a slider for the grid's pitch (a cell's width, 50 to 320px in steps of 10, 130 by default; a cell is 0.77 as tall; a remembered pitch outside that range falls back to 130), which shows the grid while it moves and re-settles every snapped module (`setSnapPitch` → `resnap` → `resettle`). Each settle remembers, beside `cell` and `box`, the box the module was put in (`placed: { x, y, w, h }`, relative to the grid's top-left), and the slider works from that, not from the cells: each module goes to the cells nearest its `placed` box, so it keeps about its size on screen rather than its count of cells, and `settleSnap` is called with `{ keepPlaced: true }` so a change of pitch never overwrites `placed`. That is why moving the slider back gives the old boxes back exactly. A module saved before `placed` existed takes its current box as `placed` the first time. `resettle` places the modules top-left first, each in the free cells nearest its box; when rounding to whole cells leaves one without a place, it tries all of them again a little smaller, by the same share, so equal modules stay equal; then all at their smallest size in rows, in order, which fits whenever any arrangement of that many at that size does; only when even that cannot fit do they share, each at the nearest cells. So no two overlap whenever the canvas holds them all at their smallest size. The work is bounded (a prefix sum of the covered cells, a few dozen passes), a few milliseconds a step, and `check-canvas` holds it to a time budget. While the slider is dragged (`input`, `preview: true`) the modules already sit where letting go leaves them, but nothing is stored (`holdStore`); the layout is written once on the `change` event, and each arrow key press is one. Two things do not use `resettle`, and overwrite `placed` as they settle: a window resize (the canvas's `resize` handler calls `settleSnap` with the saved `cell`), and a module opening onto the grid on a reload or when it is reopened (`snapOntoGrid`: the free cells nearest its saved `cell`, or, for a module the switch tiled (`layout: 'auto'`), the next free run at its smallest size). So after a reload, or a window resize, the slider returns to the boxes those left, not to earlier ones. The switch and the pitch are remembered with the space's layout (`__snap: { all, pitch }`), so a space keeps its grid. Docked and window are untouched, and a module's DOM keeps its identity through every snap, the same as through a free drag. Snap is a way of floating, not a replacement for docking: the dock's "one flexible column never leaves a gap" rule does not apply to it, and more than one snapped module may share a cell (the later one in front), as free modules may overlap. The way back is **Panel Mode** (`canvas.dockAll()`): every floating module that can be a column docks, and the canvas-level switch goes off first with nothing remembered to restore, since it would float a module again the moment it opened; a module in its own window, or one that can only float, is left alone.

**Clean up** (an entry of the Layout menu in Window Mode, `canvas.cleanUp()`; [plan-layout-menu](../plans/plan-layout-menu.md), step 2) tidies the floating modules on this canvas, in this window, in one click with no confirm and no undo. Snapped modules are tidied by `tidyCells(g, items)` in `public/snap-grid.js`: each keeps its size in cells wherever a place for it exists, at its own place when that is free, else the nearest free place of its size; only a module with no such place is made smaller, on its own. Only when the modules cannot all fit even at their smallest size does it fall back to `resettle()`. A second Clean up changes nothing. Free ones are then placed around them by `tidyBoxes(boxes, area, taken)` in `public/snap-grid.js`: fully inside the canvas less a margin and apart (`TIDY_GAP`, 4px, the same as `SNAP_GAP`), in the order they were opened, each keeping its size where it fits and made smaller on its own only when it cannot; only when one cannot fit even at the smallest size are they all made smaller together, then all at the smallest size, and only when even that cannot fit them all do they cascade, each still inside. Docked modules and modules in their own window are left alone, and nothing happens on a narrow canvas. Each module is remembered as after a drag (`layout: 'user'`). `tools/check-canvas.mjs` holds `tidyCells` and `tidyBoxes` (no overlaps, every box inside, a box that fits keeps its size, a second pass changes nothing).

## Popovers

The settings popover, the reactions tray and the overflow menu live inside the video bar's wrapper and hang off it with `bottom: calc(100% + 10px)`. They follow the bar in either state and never depend on a guessed pixel offset.

## Narrow windows

`applyLayout()` in `public/space.js` sets `narrow` on the canvas at 640px wide and below (and `compact` below 460px, `tiny` below 300px, which only shrink sizes). With `narrow` the grid is a single column and one view is shown at a time: the conference, the chat or a module takes the whole canvas, with its bar under it. The views that are not shown stay open and are hidden with `.narrow-hidden` (`syncView()` in `public/canvas.js`), so a call keeps running with the microphone and camera as they were while the chat is read. Because the microphone can be live with the conference hidden, the conference's tab carries the `in-call` class while the person is on the call (the built-in's `inCall()`, not merely while the conference is open), for the phone's tab bar to mark. `view` moves to whatever is opened.

## Rules

- **The header height is one token.** `--module-header-h` sets every module header strip so neighbouring columns line up. Nothing else sets a module header height.
- **The bottom row has one control height.** `--bar-control-h` (38px) is the height of every control in the shared bottom row: the call toolbar's buttons (`.fbtn`), the chat's icons button, input and Send, and each module's quick-add field and button. The bars pad by the same 8px, so a single-line bar is the same height in every cell and the controls line up; a multi-line chat message grows the chat cell upward from the bottom edge. Nothing else sets a control height in that row.
- **No measured heights.** `--barh` and `--floatbar-h` are gone. Anchor to the bar instead of measuring it, and let the row size itself.
- **No hand-subtracted widths.** A module that needs width gets a column; nobody writes `calc(100% - chat-w)`.
- **Colors come from tokens.** See [design-theme](../designsystem/design-theme.md).

`tools/check-canvas.mjs` enforces the first three, and runs as part of `npm run check`.

## Modules: the conference, the chat and installed modules

The conference and the chat are built-in modules (`registerBuiltin` in `public/canvas.js`), and `public/canvas.js` manages them and installed modules alike. Each module can be **docked** (a column of the grid: conference, chat, then modules in the order they opened), **floating** (draggable and resizable, in a layer on the page) or in a **window** of its own, and each has the same titlebar buttons to switch (dock or float, open in a window, close). The conference's close button leaves the call. The chat is a native module: its DOM already exists in the page, and the canvas manager moves it between the canvas, the floating layer and a popup window (a node moved to another document keeps its listeners, which is also how the whole canvas pops out). An installed module is a frame the host builds in the same places, but switching between docked and floating (`setMode` → `moveModule`) moves that same frame (and its action bar and header buttons) into the new chrome instead of rebuilding it, the same reasoning as the chat: whatever the module is holding onto (a conversation, a draft, ...) survives the switch. Only a window is a real new page — a frame cannot move between windows without reloading — so popping out still opens the module fresh, and coming back from it does too.

Docked modules set `--canvas-cols` on the canvas and each part's `grid-column`; the fixed columns together may not take more than the canvas minus a minimum for the flexible one, and shrink in step past that. The chat's width is one of those columns, remembered in `prefs.chatWidth`.

When the call is popped out, the canvas moves to the popup window and every module follows it: docked modules are inside the canvas and go with it, a floating chat is carried across, and each module is opened again in the new window, because a frame cannot move between windows without reloading and its messages arrive in the window it lives in. Closing the popout brings everything back.

The pop-out keeps its videos live. LiveKit's adaptive stream pauses a remote video it thinks nobody sees, and it judges that by the main page: `document.visibilityState` (after a few seconds hidden) and whether the element is in the main window's view. It makes an exception for Chrome's document picture-in-picture, not for a plain window, so without help a pop-out over a minimised or covered main window (an installed app's, most often) froze every video on its last picture. While a remote video is in the pop-out, `keepPoppedVideoLive()` in `public/space.js` gives its track a second watch through LiveKit's `observeElementInfo()`, saying the video is visible, in a window of its own (`pictureInPicture: true`) and at its size there (a `ResizeObserver` of the pop-out's window). `setUpPopoutWindow()` adds one for every video already attached (`keepPoppedVideosLive()`), `attachTrack()` for each camera or screen video added while popped out, and popping back in drops them all (`dropPoppedWatches()`). A local track, or one without adaptive stream, gets none. `observeElementInfo()` is LiveKit's internal method, not its public API, so an upgrade of `livekit-client` must be checked against it. `tools/check-canvas.mjs` holds the watch and where it is added and dropped.

## The conference module

The conference's titlebar (`.conference-head`, a `.mod-header` at `--module-header-h`) is the first thing in its content, above the tiles. The overlays for asides, the recall countdown and away (`#aside-overlay`, `#recall-overlay`, `#away-overlay`) are inside the same content, so they cover the conference wherever it is.

Floating or in a window, the conference is not inside the canvas, but its tiles and toolbar are styled by an ancestor `.canvas`, so the canvas manager wraps it in a `.canvas.conference-canvas` (`def.wrap`). Page code finds its elements through `$()`, which falls back to the conference element, and reads the conference's size (not the canvas's) for the `compact` and `tiny` classes. A window has its own document, so the conference registers `onWindow` to attach the idle, outside-click and key listeners there. A module's `pagehide` handler is registered only after its window has loaded, because the blank page a popup starts as also fires `pagehide` when it navigates, and that must not read as the person closing it. Moving the conference between docked, floating and a window passes `moving: true` to `onChange`, so the call keeps running. Closing it stops the call; opening it never starts one (see "The conference and the call").

The header at the top of the page is **one bar** in a space (decisions 1 to 10 of [plan-one-bar](../plans/plan-one-bar.md)), with a slim second row that is only the status strip (below). The top bar's left zone holds the space's name with its switcher, then the layout tabs (Workspace, Calendar, Map and the favorites); its right end holds the Workspace's tools (Layout, Full screen, Pop out, a module's own tools, folded into a **...** when the bar is narrow), Set up, the bell and your picture. The second row (`#subnav`, built in `public/space.js`, only in a space) is not a bar of controls wider than a phone: it holds the status strip and hides itself with nothing in it. On a phone `#subnav` is the tab bar at the foot of the page (below). The parts are in [architecture-navigation](architecture-navigation.md). Full screen and Pop out apply to the whole app (they show their exit and pop-in forms while active). Pop out moves the header (`#topbar`) and the work area (the canvas and the always-on column beside it) into the popup, so every control works where you are; the page behind shows `#away` with a "Bring the app back" button. Code that reads header parts looks inside the header element as well as the document (`byId`/`qsa` in `public/brand.js`, and `$()` in `public/space.js`). The header's links would navigate the popup away from the space, so a click on one in the popup brings the app back first and then acts on the page. Idle hides the header along with the titlebar and toolbar (`.popout:has(.canvas.idle) .topbar`).

## The work area and the always-on column

Wider than a phone, `#work` holds the canvas (the **Workspace**) and, beside it, `#side-column`, the **always-on column** ([plan-one-bar](../plans/plan-one-bar.md), decision 2 and step 6). The conference and the chat live in it in every view: they are not columns of the canvas grid, not in the Layout panel's **Show** list and not in a saved layout (their ids are still accepted in stored lists and ignored). `canvas.js` calls this mode `column` (`columnMode()` is true when there is a column element and the window is not narrow), and a page without `#side-column` (a module's own page) has no column mode at all. Pop out moves `#work`, so the column goes with the canvas.

From top to bottom the column holds: the call's titlebar (`#call-head`: its name, and a **...** with Pop out, Call position, Settings and Leave the call), the call as a strip of tiles while on it (`#side-column-strip`; the conference is moved here by `canvas.js`), the **Join the call** row out of the call (`#side-column-bar`, the same element the registry once drew in the space bar) and the chat (`#side-column-chat`). Who is here is the top bar's, not the column's. When the call is out of the column (floating, in a window of its own, or lifted into the Workspace for a screen share, which `syncShareLift` does and undoes), the column shows a slim strip (`#call-out`: the call's four controls and **Bring the call back**) and the chat has the rest.

- **Collapsing.** A handle (`#side-column-toggle`) folds the column to a 44px rail, whatever the width: the faces of the people on the call and the chat's unread count. It never closes. It is remembered per person and space in this browser (`localStorage`, `app.column.<space id>`), and opening returns to the width it had.
- **Width.** The splitter on the column's inner edge (`#side-column-splitter`) sets `prefs.chatWidth`, from `COLUMN_MIN` (220px) to the whole window, so the chat may take all of it; the Workspace then has no room and the handle folds the column away again. Arrow keys move it by 16px (Shift, 64px), Home and End jump to the least and the most, double-click or Shift+Enter restores the default.
- **The call's places.** `prefs.callPosition` is `top` (the default), `bottom` or `left`, set from Call position; `prefs.callFloat` floats the call over the Workspace instead (from the side it goes back to). **Left** puts the call beside the chat for the column's full height, and only in a column at least `CALL_LEFT_MIN` (560px) wide: in a narrower one the call sits at the top until the column is wide again, and the choice is kept (`callPlaceFor()`). Its width there is `prefs.callSideWidth` (`#side-column-call-splitter`), at least `CALL_SIDE_MIN` (240px) and leaving the chat `COLUMN_MIN`. A collapsed column and an aside allow only top or bottom. A window of its own is never remembered: it opens only from a click.
- **The 45% and 55% rule.** The whole call area is the tiles, the toolbar under them, the call's titlebar and the Join/Online row (`callChrome()` measures all but the tiles). It may take at most `CALL_MAX_SHARE` (0.55) of the column's height, so the chat always keeps at least 45% of it: the video must never squeeze the chat. The tiles' height (`prefs.stripHeight`, from `STRIP_MIN`, 100px, up to what that share leaves; `#side-column-strip-splitter`) is clamped to it with the chrome taken off (`stripMaxHeight()`, `clampStripHeight()`). An untouched strip (the default 168px) is further held to `CALL_DEFAULT_SHARE` (0.4) of the column (`stripHeightFor()`). The tiles always show (Thomas, 2026-10-14: no hiding the video): in a short window they scale down to the height they get, and the chat's share holds (`.side-column-chat`'s `min-height`). A `callTiles` preference stored before then is ignored.
- **Phones and asides.** At 640px and below the column is not drawn, and the conference and the chat go back to the phone's tabs. In an aside the column is the whole screen.
- **Where the code is.** The sizes and clamps are pure functions at the top of the column's section in `public/space.js` (`clampColumnWidth`, `stripMaxHeight`, `clampStripHeight`, `stripHeightFor`, `callPlaceFor`, `clampCallSide`, `splitterKey`); `tools/check-canvas.mjs` holds them.

## The status strip

The space's status, one line ([plan-space-status-strip](../plans/plan-space-status-strip.md), step 1; [plan-space-story-strip](../plans/plan-space-story-strip.md), steps 1 and 2). It is `#status-strip` (`role="region"`, labelled "Status"), built in `public/space.js`; its sizes are `--status-strip-h` (44px) wider than a phone, where it is the header's second row (`#subnav`, which is that tall only while the strip shows), and `--status-strip-slim-h` (28px) on a phone, where `placeSubnav()` takes it out of the registry and puts it in the header as a slim line under the top row (the sentence, and the bar as a 2px line along its foot). Its height is fixed, so its words arriving never change the header's height. It is not the module status line (`host.status.set`, `--status-bar-h`).

It holds, left to right:

- the **phase chip** (`#phase-chip`), a button whose words are the story sentence from `Phases.storyParts()` ("Booking starts tomorrow · Trip in 35 days", "Trip · day 3 of 10", "Trip finished"), after a dot in the colour of the phase today is in (`data-tint`). It has no caret: it opens the timeline sheet, not a menu. The sentence is written to its polite status only when it changes, so the presence poll never makes a screen reader speak again;
- the **journey bar**: the phases end to end, each named (with its icon, when the template gave one) where it fits, in its own tint; the past pale, today's band filled with the accent, the ones to come outlined, the main phase heavier, and a pin at today with the day of the month. It and the **days to go** after it ("35 days to Trip", "8 days left", from `Phases.countdown()`) are hidden from a screen reader, since the sentence carries them, and pressing either opens the same sheet;
- a slot kept for the facts (not built);
- the **attention count** (`#attention-count`), "N need action" or "1 needs action", only above zero. It opens a menu with a section per module (its name, then the count with its label, then its items); a pick opens the module on the canvas, or the object when the item has one.

The strip shows on every layout tab, whenever the space has phases or, in one without, once a count above zero has arrived (for the rest of that visit). It never shows for a guest, in an aside, or while Home is shown over the space. On a narrow line things give way in this order (`style.css`, between `status-strip:start` and `status-strip:end`): the facts' slot, the names in the bands (below 760px, or in a band too narrow for its name), the days to go, the bar (to 48px, then out of sight), and last the end of the sentence, each clause dropping whole, the first cut with an ellipsis. The dot never stands alone for meaning: the words are the chip's name. Colours come from the theme's tokens ([design-theme](../designsystem/design-theme.md)).

**Attention.** A module reports a count with `host.attention.set` ([api-module-sdk](../api/api-module-sdk.md)); the host sends it to `PUT /api/spaces/:id/attention/:module` and tells the page (`app:attention`), which reads `GET /api/spaces/:id/attention` again 250ms later. The page also reads it when a space loads, when the window is focused or becomes visible, and every 60 seconds while visible. The server keeps one report per space, person and module for a week (`server/attention.js`; the shape and the routes are in [api-modules](../api/api-modules.md), "What needs action"). A guest has none, and the count is the sum over the modules the person may read.

## Phases

The template lists the phases and each space holds their dates, `{ <phase id>: { start?, end? } }` ([plan-one-bar](../plans/plan-one-bar.md), decisions 7 and 8). The rule is one file, `public/phases.js`, with no document, that the server (`server/store.js`) and the pages both run (`window.Phases`), and `tools/check-phases.mjs` holds it. Its decisions:

- **Contiguous.** A boundary is the first day of the next phase, and the phase before it ends the day before. There are no gaps and no overlaps, so N phases need N-1 boundary dates. The first phase starts on the day the space was made when it has no start of its own.
- **Ends.** Only the main phase and the last phase in the template's order hold an `end`. Every other dated phase ends where the next dated one starts, and the last dated phase is open-ended unless it has an `end`.
- **Skipped phases.** A phase with no dates has no width and is skipped; its neighbours join. The first phase cannot be skipped.
- **Days.** Dates are plain `YYYY-MM-DD` days. A moment, such as the day the space was made, is turned into a day in UTC, so the server and every viewer agree; "today" is the viewer's own local day.
- **Colour.** Each phase's place in the template's list picks one of the eight tints, by name, round again after the eighth (`Phases.tintOf()`). Templates carry no colour. The sheet, Set up's rows, the status strip and a module's `host.util.phaseTimeline()` all read that one list.
- **The rule's moves.** `moveBoundary` (an end moved moves the next start, a start moved moves the previous end), `setPhaseDate`, `addDatesFor` and `skipPhase` each answer `{ ok, dates }` or `{ ok: false, problem }` with one sentence, the dates untouched; `coverMain` moves the main phase's end later and every later boundary by the same number of days. The server holds only the order of the boundaries (`problemOf`); a sheet or panel that moves through the rule never stores a gap.

**The timeline sheet** (`public/phase-sheet.js`), opened from the strip: a `<dialog>` in the editor window's look. At the top the bands laid end to end, each as wide as it is long (least width `BAND_MIN`, 56px; twelve phases scroll sideways) with a "today" line, then a row per phase with a start, an end and its length in days; an undated phase reads "Not scheduled" with **Add dates**. Nothing saves as you go: Save sends one `PATCH /api/spaces/:id` with the whole `phases` object and closes the sheet; Cancel and Escape drop the edits, asking "Discard changes?" first when dates changed. Whoever may not `PATCH` (a member) reads the same rows with the inputs disabled and sees Close alone.

## Set up

Set up is every setting of a space in one side panel on the space's own screen ([plan-setup-panel](../plans/plan-setup-panel.md); [plan-one-bar](../plans/plan-one-bar.md), decision 5), built in `public/space-setup.js`. It replaced a separate settings page. The **Set up** tool is in the top bar's right end, for owners, the admin and the space's moderators (never a member, a guest or in an aside). Pressing it puts `body.setting-up` on, shows the tool pressed, turns the space's name in the bar into an input, and opens the panel (a full-screen sheet on a phone); pressing it again is Cancel.

- **Tabs.** Each is a code id that is also its address fragment: `#space` (picture, name, description, faces in the call, launch link and its icon, the published calendar switch, and a danger section with Delete, which asks its own confirmation and does not wait for Save), `#members` (the members and Allow guests as drafts; Generate invite link and the guest link act at once), `#phases` (each phase's start and end, inline, through the same rule), `#opens-with` (labelled as the first visit: the Workspace with its Opens with switches, Calendar, Map or a shared layout), and `#modules` (a section per module with its settings for this space; no switches, since an enabled module is on in every space, and **Turn AI off**). Owners and the admin see all five; a moderator sees `#phases`, `#opens-with` and the module settings in `#modules`. A tab with nothing to show (no phases in the template, no module settings) is left out. The Lobby shows no Delete, no member switches and no calendar switch.
- **An address.** `/?setup=<space id>#<tab>` opens the panel on that tab (the id is checked against `^[A-Za-z0-9_-]{1,64}$`; an unknown tab falls back to the first the person may see). The fragment follows the tab while the panel is open and is taken out when it closes. `/?setup=lobby` opens Manage at its Spaces tab, since the Lobby has no Set up.
- **The old addresses.** The settings pages Set up replaced answer a 302 redirect to it, and the browser keeps the original fragment: `/spaces/<id>` and `/rooms/<id>` go to `/?setup=<id>`, `/module-settings?space=<id>` (or the old `?room=<id>`) to `/?setup=<id>#modules`, and `/space-settings.html`, `/roomconfig.html`, `/module-settings.html` and `/module-settings` with no id to `/` (`server/old-links.js`).
- **Saving.** Every field is a draft until Save. The panel ends in an action bar locked to its bottom (`public/action-bar.js`, below). Save sends, in order, and stops at the first refusal: `PATCH /api/spaces/:id` with only what changed (one body; a moderator's holds only `phases`, `opensWith` and `defaultLayout`), the picture (`PUT` or `DELETE /api/spaces/:id/image`), then each changed module's settings (`PUT /api/modules/:id/settings/space`). A refusal keeps the panel open on that field's tab with the server's sentence and the keyboard on the field, and says what did save. After a full save the panel closes. A tab with unsaved changes shows a dot. Escape cancels at once when nothing changed, and asks "Discard your changes?" when something did; a click outside does nothing.
- **Pure parts.** `setupTabs`, `pickTab`, `setupAddress`, `setupPatch`, `dirtyTabs`, `takeSaved`, `problemField` and `setupSaidWords` touch no document; `tools/check-nav.mjs` runs them.

**The action bar for pages.** `actionBar({ onSave, onCancel, dirty, readOnly, discardWords, saveLabel, cancelLabel, closeLabel, savedWord, submit, ids, label })` in `public/action-bar.js` is the one rule for every settings or edit surface in the app (Thomas, 2026-10-13): explicit **Save** and **Cancel** in a bar locked to the surface's bottom (the content scrolls above it; on a phone it respects the safe area), nothing saves as you go, and a click outside never closes the surface. It is the page's twin of the editor window's button row. `el` goes last in a flex column whose content scrolls (`.action-surface`), and `bindEscape(surface)` makes Escape Cancel. Save stays off until `dirty()` says something changed and reads "Saved" afterwards (then Cancel reads **Close**). `onSave()` does the save and returns `false`, or throws, when it did not go through (the throw's message is shown); anything else is a save. `onCancel()` closes the surface with the edits dropped, after `requestCancel()` asks `discardWords` ("Discard changes?" by default) with **Keep editing** and **Discard** when `dirty()` is true. `readOnly: true` shows no Save, only Close. It returns `{ el, refresh, save, requestCancel, bindEscape, say, setBusy, saved, isAsking, saveButton, cancelButton }`; call `refresh()` after any edit. The timeline sheet, the Set up panel and the layout save and icon sheets in the space page use it; the look is `.action-bar` in `style.css`, and `tools/check-action-bar.mjs` holds every surface to it. It is for the app's own pages, not an SDK piece: a module's forms use the editor window.

## Home

Home is the environment's page that lists its spaces, reached by the Home link ([plan-home-lobby](../plans/plan-home-lobby.md)). It uses the page's whole width in three columns, `[Chat] [Spaces] [Modules]` (`.home` in `public/style.css`, markup in `public/space.html`):

- **Chat**, on the left, the full height under the top bar, staying put while the page scrolls (`#home-chat`). `space.js` moves the one `#chat` into it while Home shows with no space entered, for someone signed in who may read chat (never a guest), and gives it its own width (`prefs.homeChatWidth`, 360px by default, resized with the splitter `#home-chat-splitter`; `--home-chat-w`).
- **Spaces**, in the middle: who is around above the cards (`#whos-around`), then the heading with the home word and, for owners and the admin, the button that makes a space and takes them into it with Set up open, then the space cards.
- **Modules**, on the right (`#home-modules`): the tiles that modules provide, filled by `public/dashboard.js`, headed with the module word. It is not drawn with no tiles. Below 1200px the Modules go under the space cards, and at 760px and below everything is one column.

**The Lobby is Home's hidden backing.** The Lobby's record (space id `lobby`) stays, but a signed-in person never enters it: it has no card, no entry in the space switcher and no Set up, and every old way in (`/#join=lobby`, a reload that remembers it, an aside that falls back to it, an invitation to it) lands on Home. Home's chat is the Lobby's chat: its history, its sends through the same routes, and what others say live from `GET /api/spaces/lobby/chat/stream`, since Home holds no call ([api-modules](../api/api-modules.md), "Chat routes"). The stream is open whenever the page is signed in and may read chat, and is drawn into Home's chat while it shows, else counted as an unread number on the Home link; each connection starts with `ready`, and every `ready` after the first reads the history again to catch up on what was missed. While Home is shown over a space the person is still in, the same `#chat` moves into Home's column and the space's messages are kept aside until **Back**. A guest link made for the Lobby still enters it as before, and everything else that reads the record (OBS, tokens, Stream, Manage) is unchanged.

## Phones

**The space bar on a phone** ([plan-phone-space-bar](../plans/plan-phone-space-bar.md), GitHub #160). At 640px and below, wherever the bar is (a phone, a narrow window, a narrow pop-out), it reads: the module tabs that fit, Join, Online's count, Rejoin call in an aside, the **…** and Leave. `phoneTabs()` in `public/nav-bar.js` decides which tabs show (each at least `TAB_MIN`, 56px, as the stylesheet's `flex: 1 0 56px`; the shown view's tab always stays) and `phoneFold()` which tools go into the **…**: every right-zone tool except those marked `phone: 'bar'` (Leave, Rejoin call), and those marked `phone: 'popout'` (Full screen, Pop out) only in a popped-out window, where Full screen reads its `activeLabel` ("Exit full screen") while on and Pop out its `short` ("Pop it back in"). The **…** holds the folded tabs, a divider, then the tools; its badge adds their counts, and when the Conference's tab is folded while on the call it carries the call's green dot (red while the microphone is live). Join takes its own width, so one tab fewer shows rather than the tabs squeezing. In a narrow pop-out the bar sits on the header's second row and slides away with the header when idle, and Online shows its count. Online's list opens upward. A module's tools from `host.nav.set` are always entries in the **…** on a phone; the SDK is unchanged. `phone`, `short` and `activeLabel` are registry fields for the page's own tools only. After **Pop it back in**, the main bar drops the pop-out's fold. `tools/check-nav.mjs` holds `phoneTabs`, `phoneFold` and `TAB_MIN`.

Below 640px (the width `applyLayout()` calls `narrow`) the space page is a phone layout, in one `@media (max-width: 640px)` block at the end of `public/style.css`: a slim header, then the canvas, then the space bar as a tab bar (icon over name, the accent color for an open module; Full screen and Pop out are hidden, Leave stays). `public/space.js` moves `#subnav` out of the header and after the canvas on a phone (and back when the window widens), so the bar is the last thing in the page's column and the call toolbar sits directly above it; nothing measures or pads for it. Its own bottom padding is the safe-area inset, which keeps the icons clear of a browser's bottom bar. `body.in-space` is `position: fixed; top: 0` with `height: 100dvh` (no `inset: 0`, no `100%`): see the measurements below. The tab bar switches the view: the highlighted tab (`.on`) is the view being shown, tapping another tab shows it, and tapping never closes a module or hangs up (the hang-up button and the conference's own close are the way out of the call). The other views stay open but hidden, so the call keeps running, and because the microphone can be live while the conference is hidden, the Conference tab carries a dot while you are in the call: green with the microphone off, red while it is live (`reflectMic()` in `public/space.js` sets `data-mic` on `#modules-menu`, and `canvas.js` puts `in-call` on the tab while the conference's `inCall()` says so). Who is here is a count in the tab bar, before Leave. On entering, the first tab shown is the first module `restore()` opened, whichever it is; the conference only when it comes first.

## Remembered layouts

Each space remembers its own layout in the browser (`localStorage`, key `app.canvas.<space id>`; `public/brand.js` moves the old `app.panels`, `app.panels.<id>` and `tavern.panels.<id>` keys to these once on load, only where the new key is empty): the modules that were open when it was last used (`__open`, ids in column order) and, per module, its mode, docked width, floating box and window size. `app.canvas` alone (moved from `app.panels`) is what earlier versions kept for every space, and is the starting point for a space with nothing saved.

- `createCanvas()` in `public/canvas.js` writes the open list from `update()` (`snapshot()`), so any change of modules is remembered. Nothing is written while the manager is suspended: from the start of a space's teardown (`suspend()`, called first in the Disconnected handler) until the next join has restored its modules (`restore()`), and while a pop-out reopens the modules. Closing every module on the way out therefore never becomes the layout. An aside has no space id and remembers nothing.
- `restore()` runs after entering a space connects. What it opens is decided in this order ([plan-entering](../plans/plan-entering.md), "What opens on entering"):
  1. a fresh request to open one module on one object (from the dashboard, within 20 seconds);
  2. the person's remembered layout (`__open`), never for a guest;
  3. the space's own `opensWith`;
  4. the environment's list for a new space (`spaceDefaults.opensWith`, from its template);
  5. the product default: the chat and every module on in the space for this person, in the Modules menu's order, without the conference. A guest gets the conference and the chat.

  Steps 2 to 5 are `whatOpens()` in `public/opens-with.js`, a pure function with no document, so `tools/check-canvas.mjs` runs it in node. A list from step 3 or 4 is filtered to what this person may open here (`canOpenHere()`: the conference and the chat by their own rule, any other module by being on in this space for them); a list that is set but holds nothing openable opens nothing, rather than falling through to the next step. Both lists come from `GET /api/modules/for-space` (`opensWith` and `spaceDefaultsOpensWith`). A module the person no longer has (a permission taken away, a module turned off) is skipped. Switching the conference off before leaving is remembered like any other change, so the next visit has no conference; hanging up leaves it open, so the next visit shows it out of the call. Whatever opens, the conference among it opens out of the call.
- Set up's **Opens with** tab (`public/space-setup.js`) writes the space's own list (`opensWith`, through `PATCH /api/spaces/:id`) without entering, so an owner, the admin or a moderator can choose what a first visit opens. It lists the modules as switches (`public/switch-list.js`); a saved id that cannot open now is kept, and the tab says why.

## Saved layouts

A saved layout is a named copy of the canvas's arrangement, kept on the server so it follows a person to any browser ([plan-saved-layouts](../plans/plan-saved-layouts.md); GitHub #161). The routes and the stored shape are in [api-modules](../api/api-modules.md), "Saved layouts"; the panel is in [architecture-navigation](architecture-navigation.md), "The module chooser (Layout)".

- **Capture.** `captureLayout()` on the canvas reads what is open with the pure `layoutOf(modules, area, snap)` in `public/layouts.js`: the modules in column order (docked, then floating in the order they were opened), each docked width in pixels (none for the flexible column), each floating box as fractions of the canvas, whether it snaps, and the canvas's snap switch and pitch. A module in its own window is saved as the mode it had on the canvas.
- **Load.** `loadLayout(layout)` works out the places with the pure `placeLayout(layout, area, canOpen)`: fractions to pixels, kept at least the smallest size and on screen, and which ids are left out (not on in the space, not installed, or not readable by this person; the panel says so with `leftOutNote()`, and the stored layout is not changed). It closes the open modules the layout does not name, except the conference while this person is on the call; brings a module in its own window back to the canvas; opens or moves the rest; settles snapped ones onto the grid at the saved pitch (`resettle()`); and runs the docked ones through `fitDock()`, so those a narrower window has no column for wait (see "How many columns fit"). The result becomes the person's remembered layout for the space.
- **The default layout.** An owner, the admin or the space's moderator may name the layout a first visit shows (`defaultLayout`: a shared layout's id, `calendar` or `map`; [plan-layout-views](../plans/plan-layout-views.md), step 5). It is independent of `opensWith`, the Workspace's own list of modules: changing one leaves the other. The server resolves it for the viewer (`firstVisit` in `GET /api/spaces/:id/layouts` and `GET /api/modules/for-space`: `{ layout, opensWith }`, where `layout` falls back to `workspace` when the saved one no longer resolves). On a first visit, with no remembered layout, and for a guest on every visit, the page shows that layout (a shared one through the same `loadLayout()`, places included; Calendar or Map as its composite); after that the person's remembered layout wins. Set up's **Opens with** tab (`public/space-setup.js`) chooses it.
- `LAYOUT_LIMITS` in `public/layouts.js` mirrors the server's. The canvas also exposes `isNarrow()`, and `createCanvas()` takes `onMenu(open)` so the space page refreshes its Layouts section as the panel opens. No SDK change.
- At 640px and below, in an aside, and for a guest with no shared layouts, there is no Layouts section. `tools/check-layouts.mjs` holds the server's rules and the pure functions; `tools/check-canvas.mjs` and `tools/check-nav.mjs` the canvas and the panel.

## The conference and the call

Being in the space and being on the call are separate. The page stays connected to the space's LiveKit session for the chat, the modules and who is here (chat travels over LiveKit's data channel), and sends and receives audio and video only while the person is on the call. Entering a space never joins the call, for members and guests alike (Thomas, 2026-09-30): only a click does.

- The conference is a native module (`id: 'conference'`, `order: -1`, `flex: true`, docked only). Its switch always reads its name (the template's or environment's name for it), never a closed label, and like every switch only shows and hides it (Thomas, 2026-09-30). Its `onChange` in `public/space.js` shows **Not in a call** (`setNoCall(true)`) when it opens out of the call, and runs `stopCall()` when it closes, so switching it off, or its titlebar's x, hangs up and nobody is left on the call with the conference hidden. Nothing in `onChange` starts the call.
- **Not in a call** (`#no-call`) lists who is on the call now under **Currently on the call** (`syncWhoIsInCall()`: the call's own participants whose `call` attribute is not `off`, with their picture in this space, else their profile picture, else their first letter; "No one is on the call yet. Be the first to join." when nobody is), then **Join the call** (`#no-call-join`), then the microphone note and `#install-hint` (`#call-hint`). A refusal of the join is said in `#no-call-error`. The toolbar's phone is red on the call (hang up) and green off it (join, titled "Join the call"); both it and **Join the call** call `phoneButton()`.
- `startCall()` is the join. It first asks `POST /api/call/join` (`{ space }`, or `{ guest }` for a guest), the calls cap ([architecture-overview](architecture-overview.md), "Spaces and calls"). A refusal shows the server's own sentence, and anything without one (no answer, a 500 page, a proxy's 502) shows "Could not reach the server to join the call. Try again in a moment."; nothing is asked of the microphone. Then it sets the `call` attribute to `on`, subscribes to everyone on the call and publishes the microphone. Every wait after the check is followed by `stopped()`: `stopCall()` counts `callGeneration` up, so a hang-up, the conference switched off or a lost connection while the join is still waiting makes it back out, unpublishing and releasing what it got. A second join waits for one still backing out, so two never share the microphone. `stopCall()` unpublishes, unsubscribes, removes the tiles and sets `off`.
- The page connects with `autoSubscribe: false`. A participant attribute, `call` (`on` or `off`), says whether a person is on the call. Every player and guest token starts with `off` (`mintToken()` in `server/index.js`); `connectAndSetup()` says `off` again in case a token ever says otherwise, and only a join sets `on`. An OBS viewer's token carries no attribute and is hidden. A tile exists only for a person whose attribute is not `off`, and only while I am on the call myself (`shownInCall()`). `GET /api/presence` reports `inCall` per person, and the aside route (`POST /api/asides`) refuses anyone who is not on the call.
- **A pull keeps you on the call** (Thomas, 2026-09-30). Being pulled into an aside or a private conversation, or back (Pull participants back's countdown, Rejoin call, following the others back on `aside-return`), goes through `reconnectTo(spaceId, text, { keepCall: true })`, which reads `inCall` before disconnecting and hands `join()` and `connectAndSetup()` `joinCall`. With it, the conference is opened and `startCall()` runs, through the same check and microphone rules as a click; out of the call before, you land in **Not in a call**. Going somewhere yourself (entering, an invitation, a module opened from the dashboard) never passes `keepCall`. `tools/check-canvas.mjs` holds which moves are pulls. **Rejoin call** shows only while the current space is an aside with an `origin`, the space it came from. `aside-started` lists the aside on the page at once, before `GET /api/presence` answers, but carries no origin, so that entry is marked `pending`; a pulled person's `join()` that finds it keeps it only until presence answers, when `adoptPendingSpace()` puts presence's record in its place, with its origin, and redraws the space bar, whose name then reads "<space> · Aside" (`updateCrumb()`; "Private" for a private conversation). `tools/check-canvas.mjs` runs the page's `DataReceived` handler against a stand-in call to hold this.
- Push to talk does nothing while not on the call (`onKey()` checks `inCall`); letting go still always stops talking.
- **The muted mark** on a tile is the microphone publication's own state, `micMarkedMuted(pub)`: muted when there is no microphone publication or it is muted. `updateMuted()` reads it on `TrackMuted` and `TrackUnmuted`, and also whenever a microphone comes or goes: `TrackPublished`, `TrackUnpublished`, `TrackSubscribed`, `LocalTrackPublished` and `LocalTrackUnpublished`. That matters for someone who joins after you: they set `call` to `on` before publishing their microphone, so their tile is made marked muted, and a microphone published unmuted sends no `TrackUnmuted`. A late joiner's own view is right for the same reason, since the publication carries its state. `tools/check-canvas.mjs` runs the call's event handlers against a stand-in call to hold this.
- **Away** (`setAway()`) pauses the microphone and camera, remembering which were on (`awayRestoreMic`, `awayRestoreCam`) and turning only those back on at **Back**, and also stops you hearing others. What you hear is off while `hearingOff()` is true (`prefs.deafened` or `isAway`): `applyHearing()` sets every audio element where the canvas is (`canvasDoc()`, so the pop-out too), `setAway()` calls it straight after changing `isAway`, before waiting on the microphone and camera, and `attachTrack()` mutes a remote audio element attached later by the same rule. While away nothing opens the microphone: `toggleMic()` refuses to turn it on and says "away: come back to turn your microphone on", push to talk's key is still taken but opens nothing (`onKey()`), and `startCall()` publishes it muted, setting `awayRestoreMic` so **Back** turns it on (unless push to talk is on). The camera is held off too: `toggleCam()` refuses to turn it on and says "away: come back to turn your camera on" (the button, **V** and `prefs.camKey` all go through it), and `restartCamera()`, which a camera or quality change in Settings or the profile calls, only sets `awayRestartCam` while away; `setAway(false)` applies that restart after turning the camera back on; if the camera stays off, `toggleCam()` applies it the next time the camera is turned on, since turning it on alone would open the device it had before. `keepCameraOffWhileAway()` and `keepMicOffWhileAway()` turn off a camera or microphone that came on during a wait after going away (a quick **Back** and away again). The state (`isAway`, `awayRestoreMic`, `awayRestoreCam`, `awayMessage`, `awayRestartCam`) is declared beside `inCall` at the top of `space.js`, because `hearingOff()` is read from page load.
- **The away mark** is carried as two participant attributes beside `call`, so the call service keeps it and someone who joins, reloads or reconnects later sees it: `away` (`on` or `off`; a participant without it counts as `off`) and `awayMessage` (the message, trimmed and at most 200 characters, `''` for a plain Away). `awayAttributes()` builds them from `isAway` and `awayMessage`, and `syncAwayAttributes()` sends them when they differ from what the call service holds. `setAttributes` changes only the keys it is given, so away never touches `call` and the reverse. They are said in `sendAway()`, again in `connectAndSetup()` (away before connecting), with `call: 'on'` in `startCall()` (a join while away), and on `Reconnected` by `syncCallAttributes()`, which sends `call` (`on` if I am on the call, else `off`) together with them, because a full reconnect joins the call service afresh with the token's attributes and others would drop my tile and the server stop counting me; `stopCall()` sends `{ call: 'off', away: 'off', awayMessage: '' }`, since away ends with the call. On the receiving end, `showAway()` draws a tile's mark from the person's attributes (my own from my own state), called by `tileFor()` when a tile is made and by `ParticipantAttributesChanged` when `away` or `awayMessage` changes. For one release the old one-time data message (topic `away`, `{ type: 'away', on, message }`) is still sent by `sendAway()` and read in `DataReceived`, for pages still on the script before the attributes; it is to be removed in the next release. `tools/check-canvas.mjs` holds the hearing, the microphone and camera buttons, the join, the attributes on both ends and the old message; `tools/check-module-host.mjs` holds push to talk while away.
- The hang-up button leaves the call and keeps the conference open, showing **Not in a call**. Leave space, at the right of the space bar, disconnects. Closing the module in a pop-out window first brings the canvas back to the page. The conference's close button is titled "Close" out of the call and "Leave the call and close" on it.
- The module switches are in the space bar's **Layout** panel, under **Show**, not the toolbar, so they work with the conference closed.
- The environment's conference switch (`conferenceEnabled`) holds for everyone, owners and the admin included: `conferenceAllowed()` in `public/opens-with.js` checks the switch before the permission, and the conference's `allowed()`, the **Modules** list and the Open with popover all use it. The status line reads "in <space>" whether or not the person is on the call.
- The "See and join the conference" permission is enforced in the token: without it the token cannot publish or subscribe, and the module cannot be opened.

## Docked installed modules

An installed module can dock as a column after the chat. `public/canvas.js` adds a `.module.module-docked` element to the canvas holding a `.dock-resize` handle and a `.mod-content` with a host-drawn `.mod-header` (at `--module-header-h`) and the module's frame. The content always spans both rows of the grid: a module draws its add actions in the titlebar's **+** and its counts in the status line at the foot of its own window, and there is no bar in the bottom row (the old `host.bar.set` is folded into the **+**). The canvas sets `--canvas-cols` and places each column, so the template is one column per docked module, with the conference or the first module flexible. Dragging a column's left edge changes only that column's width.

On a narrow window one view is shown at a time (see the narrow rules above). A module opens docked, laid out as the chat is (the canvas gets `module-open`, set by `syncDock`, beside `chat-open` and `conference-open`): it fills the canvas, its action bar below it. A module that cannot dock still floats. Its titlebar buttons are 44px wide for touch, at the shared header height.

The next docked module needs nothing here: it is the same element with the next column number.
