# Navigation Architecture

**Audience:** developers changing the header on any page of Collaborator (`public/brand.js`, `public/space.js`, `public/style.css`), or adding a control to it.

The header is two rows, and each row is three zones. The rows are about different things and never borrow from each other; a control belongs to exactly one zone, chosen by what it is about and what it does, never by where it happens to fit.

## The primary nav: the system

`.topbar`, built by `renderTopbar()` in `public/brand.js`, the same on every page. The markup it writes is only what is not a tool (the logo, the crumb, the status, the menu button); the middle and right zones are drawn from the registry (below).

| Zone | Class | Holds |
|---|---|---|
| Left | `.nav-left` (`.brand`) | the logo and server name (the home link), the crumb saying where you are (`#topbar-crumb`, `setTopbarLocation()`), the status line |
| Middle | `.nav-middle` (`#core-nav`) | the core navigation, one group: Spaces (`#spaces-link`, order 1) and each module's own page (`page-<module>`, orders 11 and up, class `module-nav-link`, hidden in a space, where the space's own module selector is the way in) |
| Right | `.nav-right` (`.links`) | the system's actions and information, three groups with a divider between, in this order: the system (the light or dark switch `theme-mode-switch`, not on the host console; Manage `#admin-link`), the session (Call settings in a space, the time `#topbar-clock` on the server's clock setting), and last you (`#whoami-link`, group `you`, order 999; see "Your picture and the account menu" below). There is no separate Sign out button, and no Install button (`#install-link` was removed); both are in the account menu. The host console hides this zone |

### Your picture and the account menu

`#whoami-link` is a `<button>` holding your picture (`#whoami-img`) and your name (`#whoami`). A long name is cut short with an ellipsis at 10em; on a wider screen it has the title "Account" and `aria-haspopup="menu"`, and its accessible name is the full name followed by "Account" (`aria-labelledby`). On a phone `paintWhoami()` makes it a label in the header's menu instead (see "Phones keep the meaning, not the place" below), and repaints it whenever the window crosses the phone width. `fillWhoami()` in `renderTopbar()` fills the picture and name on any page that has not filled them itself, so every page shows them. With no account (a guest's page, `/api/me` answering 401) the button is hidden, and so there is no account menu.

On a wider screen a click opens the page's shared menu (`openHostMenu()` in `public/host-menu.js`, see [architecture-module-window](architecture-module-window.md), "Overflow") under the button, with the entries built by `accountMenuItems()` in `public/brand.js`, which call `viewProfile()`, `installFromPrompt()` and `signOut()` (the phone's menu entries call the same three):

- **View profile** dispatches a cancelable `app:open-profile` event on the document first. A page that can show the profile without leaving takes it (`preventDefault()`): the space page does, and opens `/profile` in its overlay so the call keeps running, bringing a popped-out app back first. Otherwise the menu goes to `/profile`, keeping the query of a page opened over a call (`?from=space…`) so the profile keeps its **Back to** button; on `/profile` itself it only closes the menu.
- **Install as an app** is there only while `installPromptEvent` is set: Chrome and Edge fire `beforeinstallprompt` when the page can be installed, `brand.js` keeps the event, and the entry calls its `prompt()`. A browser that never fires it (Firefox, Safari) never shows the entry; the space page's `describeInstall()` gives that browser's own way instead, in `#install-hint` under **Join the call** and `#install-note` in the call's settings. A guest has no account menu, and so no Install.
- **Sign out** forgets this browser's light or dark choice (`forgetThemeMode()`) and sends the top window to `/logout` when it is the same origin, so a page opened over a call signs the whole window out, not only its frame.

Manage (`#admin-link`) likewise keeps the `?from=space…` query of a page opened over a call, so it keeps its way back.

## The secondary nav: the space

`.subnav`, built in `public/space.js`, only in a space (`body.in-space`). All three zones are drawn from the registry except the space's name, which is markup; the right zone is the bar a module's tools go into.

| Zone | Class | Holds |
|---|---|---|
| Left | `.nav-left` | the space's name (`#space-name`, set by `updateCrumb()`, with the whole name as its title), then the module chooser (`module-chooser`, an `element` tool: the **Modules** button `#modules-toggle`, in the environment's word for modules, and its list `#modules-menu`) |
| Middle | `.nav-middle` (`#subnav-middle`) | the space's information and navigation: who is here (`who-here`, an `element` tool, `public/space-people.js`); a module may register here too |
| Right | `.nav-right` (`.subnav-tools`) | the space's actions: Dock all (`#dock-all`, every floating module back beside the call), the canvas-level snap (`#snap-all`) and its grid slider (`#snap-size`), Full screen (`#fullscreen-toggle`), Pop out (`#popout`), Pull participants back (`#recall-button`, during an aside), Rejoin call (`#rejoin-call`, in an aside); then, after a divider, a module's own groups; then the **…** (`#subnav-more`, order 998, shown only while something is folded) and Leave (`#leave-space`, order 999, the same `right-from-bracket` icon as Sign out) last |

**Widgets.** Thomas calls the live pieces in the two bars *widgets*: they show something that changes as it happens, not an action. Who is here is the first. (The dashboard's cards, called widgets today, are to be renamed *tiles*.) A widget is an `element` tool like any other; the registry does not treat it differently.

### The module chooser

One button in place of a switch per module (Thomas, 2026-09-30). `public/canvas.js` fills `#modules-menu` from `update()`: on a wider screen a list of switches drawn by `public/switch-list.js`, the conference, the chat and the space's modules in that order, each on while its module is shown (a module open but hidden behind another view on a narrow canvas reads off, and its switch shows it). The list is rebuilt in place on every change, keeping the keyboard on the switch that had it, and stays open while switches are flipped; the button, Escape or a click elsewhere closes it (by the click's `composedPath()`, since a flipped switch is already out of the document). The button carries the total unread count. On a phone the same `#modules-menu` becomes the tab bar (`subnav-modules`) and the button is not drawn; `placeSubnav()` in `public/space.js` decides by the width of the window the header is in, so a narrow popped-out window gets the tab bar too, and re-decides when that window crosses 640 px or the header moves between windows.

`public/switch-list.js` is the one piece for every list of module switches: the chooser, the space list's **Open with** popover (`toggleJoinWith()` in `public/space.js`), and space settings' **Opens with** and module list (`public/space-settings.js`). `switchListHtml(items, attr)` and `switchRowHtml(item, attr)` draw `{ id, icon, name, on, note?, badge?, disabled? }` as a real checkbox with `role="switch"` and `data-<attr>="<id>"`, so a screen reader says on or off and Space flips it; `wireSwitchList(list)` adds Enter to flip and the Up, Down, Home and End keys to move. Each page listens for `change` or `click` on the list itself.

### Who is here

`public/space-people.js`, created by `createWhoHere()` in `public/space.js` and registered as `who-here` (group `people`, `fold: false`, with `fit`). It reads the page's own LiveKit connection: the local participant first, then the remote ones by name, never a hidden one (an OBS view); a person is on the call when their `call` attribute is not `off` (for yourself, the page's own `inCall`). The call's events keep it live, and `space.js` calls `refresh()` when its own call state changes. The button shows up to four square portraits side by side (the space's picture, then the profile picture, then the first letter; rounded corners, a thin `--border`, no ring for you, who are first), "+N" for the rest in a square of the same size, and words from `hereLabel()`: `names` ("Just you", "You and Alex", "You, Alex and Sam", "You and 4 others", first names cut at 16 characters) or `count` ("5 in this space", the environment's word, or "aside"). With no portraits, or where the stylesheet hides them (a phone's tab bar), it shows an icon and `hereWords()`'s short form, "5 here" or "Just you". Its title and accessible name say "N people in this space, M on the call". The list is headed "In this space (N)" and gives each person's portrait and name, "(you)", and the camera with "on the call". The only colour in it is the camera mark (`--ok`), never alone. The list under it (`#who-here-list`) is placed by `place()` inside the window the header is in: under the button when the bar is in the window's top half, above it in a phone's bottom tab bar, no wider or taller than the window leaves, scrolling. It closes on the button, Escape, a click elsewhere or focus leaving; opened from the keyboard it takes focus, and Escape gives it back to the button. On a phone it is a compact count in the tab bar, between the module tabs and Leave. The pure parts `facesThatFit()`, `peopleIn()` and `hereWords()` are held by `tools/check-nav.mjs`; `hereLabel()` is not yet.

### The fold: the "…"

The space bar never lets its zones overlap (Thomas, 2026-09-30). `fitSecondary()` in `public/nav-bar.js` runs on the next frame after any redraw, resize, font load or change in the left or middle zone's size (a `ResizeObserver` in whichever window the bar is in now). It measures the bar with nothing folded (`nav-measuring`, so the name counts at its full width), and then:

1. A middle-zone tool with `fit(avail)` is told the width the middle may take (`middleWidth()`: centred between the wider of the left and right zones, or beside a name too long to centre on), and shrinks to it. Who is here gives way in steps: the names with every portrait; else the count ("5 in this space") with as many portraits as fit; else the short count alone ("5 here"). The names are kept only while every portrait fits, so words give way before faces. So the middle gives way before anything folds.
2. `foldSteps()` lists the right zone's tools in folding order: by band, each band from its end, the secondary band (Pop out, then Full screen), a module's own, the core band (the snap switch with its grid slider, then Dock all), then the utility band (Rejoin call, then Pull participants back). An `element` tool with no click of its own (the grid slider) folds with the tool before it. The last band (Leave), a tool with `fold: false` (the **…** itself) and anything in the left or middle zone never fold.
3. `foldCount()` gives how many steps must fold for the right zone to fit beside the others; those tools get `nav-folded`, and the **…** shows. Only once everything that can fold has does the space's name shrink, cut short with an ellipsis.

The **…** opens the page's shared menu (`openHostMenu()` in `public/host-menu.js`) with the folded tools in the bar's order: a toggle as a checkbox entry (`checked`, `role="menuitemcheckbox"`, `aria-checked`), a count as a `badge`. Picking one clicks the tool's own element, so it does exactly what it does in the bar; the grid slider has no entry. A count on any folded tool shows on the **…**. After a pick, focus goes to the tool if the bar now has room for it, else stays on the **…**, else goes to the first control in the left zone (the Modules button); the same when the **…** disappears while it had focus. A divider with nothing shown before it is hidden. On a phone none of this applies: the bar is the tab bar.

`foldSteps()`, `foldCount()`, `middleCentred()` and `middleWidth()` are pure and exported on `nav`; `tools/check-nav.mjs` holds them.

## The registry: one drawing path

`public/nav-bar.js` is what both bars draw from. A tool is a registration, `{ id, bar, zone, icon, label, title?, order?, group?, groupOrder?, href? | onClick, visible?, toggleable?, active?, badge? }`, and the registry draws it and owns its look: an icon button (`.icon-link`) everywhere but the primary nav's middle zone, where a tool is the core navigation's icon and name (`.core-link`); a tool with `href` is a real link. Five things only the page's own tools may add: `element` (an element the registry places and orders but does not draw: your picture, the clock, the snap slider, the module chooser, who is here), `labelled` (a small text button with its icon: Pull participants back), `activeIcon` (what a toggle shows while on), `fold: false` (never folded into the space bar's **…**) and, for a secondary middle-zone tool, `fit(avail)` (called with the width the middle may take before anything folds; the tool shrinks to it).

- **Groups and bands.** Tools sit in groups, a divider between groups that show something; groups sort by `groupOrder` (the smallest any tool of the group names, or its first tool's order), tools by `order`, ties by registration. The bands are 1-10 core, 11-50 secondary, 51-100 utility, 101-998 a module's own, 999 last, so the system's tools are ahead of a module's without anyone coordinating numbers. `tools/check-nav.mjs` holds these rules.
- **Visibility and state.** `visible` is a boolean or a function (Rejoin call reads whether the current space is an aside; who is here whether the page is connected to a space; the **…** whether anything is folded; the phone menu's Install as an app whether the browser offered to install); a tool that leaves it out is shown, and other code may toggle its element's own `hidden` (Manage, which each page shows once it knows the viewer is an admin; your picture, hidden for a guest). `isShown(tool, hiddenOf)` (exported, and on `nav`) is the one rule for what counts as shown: a tool's `visible` when it has one, else whether its element is not hidden. Dividers go only between groups with something shown, and the registry watches the `hidden` of each tool without `visible` (a `MutationObserver`) and redraws its bar when that changes, so no divider is left before an empty group (a guest's header, or a guest's menu on a phone). A `toggleable` tool carries `active` and is changed by `nav.setActive(id, on)`, a count by `nav.setBadge(id, n)`, both in place, never by registering again. `nav.draw(bar)` redraws after something a `visible` function reads has changed.
- **A module's tools** arrive through `host.nav.set` (module-host.js, the `nav.set` handler) under the module's own namespace (`<module>:<id>`, its own group, orders clamped into 101-998), are drawn while the module is open on the canvas and taken out when it closes or the module is unmounted, and click back as the module's `nav` event. They go in the secondary bar. The primary bar takes a module's registration only for a system-wide tool (`system: true`) from a module the admin allowed into the primary nav (`surfaces.page.nav`), and only into the right zone; anything else is refused with a clear error. The contract is in [api-module-sdk](../api/api-module-sdk.md), "Registering into the nav bars".

## Rules

- **The middle is centred on the row.** Each row is a grid of `minmax(0, 1fr) auto minmax(0, 1fr)`, so the middle zone sits in the same place whatever the left and right zones hold; the left zone justifies its content to the start, the right to the end.
- **Two builders, one registry, no others.** `brand.js` builds the primary nav and `space.js` the secondary; both register their controls into `nav-bar.js`, which draws them. A page that wants a control in the header registers a tool, never appends markup of its own.
- **Phones keep the meaning, not the place.** At 640px and below (`max-width: 640px`) the primary nav is a row of the logo, the crumb and a menu button (`#nav-toggle`, labelled **Menu**); the middle zone is left empty and the registry draws the middle zone's tools into the menu (the right zone's element), then the right zone's, and back when the window widens (the registry watches the width itself). The fold is the pure `phoneZones(byZone)` (exported, and on `nav`, held by `tools/check-nav.mjs`): each zone becomes a list of runs arranged on their own, so the middle's tools stay ahead of the right's whatever their orders. Nothing stays in the bar: the menu holds Spaces, the light or dark switch, Manage, Call settings in a space, the time, and last, after a divider, you. There your picture (`#whoami-link`) is a label with your name, not a button: `brand.js` takes it out of the Tab order, sets `aria-disabled` and drops its account menu. **View profile** (`account-profile`), **Install as an app** (`account-install`) and **Sign out** (`account-sign-out`) follow it as the menu's own entries: page tools in the right zone's `you` group whose `visible` is true only on a phone and only while your picture shows, so a guest has none of them; Install also only while the browser offers to install. They call the same `viewProfile()`, `installFromPrompt()` and `signOut()` as the account menu. Opening the menu redraws the bar (so these follow a page that hid your picture) and puts focus on its first entry; Tab moves through the entries and back to the menu button; Escape closes it and returns focus to the button; focus leaving the menu and its button closes it. Crossing the phone width closes the menu, and an account menu open when the window narrows to a phone closes too; focus goes back to what had it if that is still shown, else to the menu button on a phone or the right zone's first entry on a wider screen. The menu panel sits inside the screen's right edge (the right zone's own negative margin is cancelled there). The secondary nav becomes the tab bar at the bottom of the page: the module chooser's list is the bar (a tab per module) and its button is not drawn, who is here is a compact count before Leave, the space's name is not drawn, and of the right zone only Leave stays; nothing folds into the **…** there (see [architecture-canvas](architecture-canvas.md), "Phones").
- **Popped out, the header goes too.** Pop out moves the header with the canvas into the popup, so every control works where you are; the registry keeps the zone elements, not selectors, so it follows them. See [architecture-canvas](architecture-canvas.md).
